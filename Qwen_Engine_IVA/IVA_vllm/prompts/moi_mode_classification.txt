Rules:

1. Only classify a mode when the evidence SPECIFICALLY and AFFIRMATIVELY
   describes THIS gene's own inheritance pattern. Do NOT classify from an
   incidental, negated, or differential-diagnosis mention of "dominant" or
   "recessive" (e.g. "not consistent with dominant inheritance", "unlike
   gene X, which is dominant", a sentence about a different gene entirely).
   If you are not confident the evidence is actually talking about THIS
   gene's own mode, output UNKNOWN rather than guessing.

2. Explicitly check whether the evidence describes a DOMINANT-NEGATIVE
   mechanism (e.g. "poison subunit", "interferes with the wild-type
   protein", "acts in a dominant-negative fashion", "multimer disruption").
   A dominant-negative mechanism is genuinely AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT — but,
   unlike haploinsufficiency, it does NOT require the gene to be
   LoF-intolerant. A gene reported with a dominant-negative mechanism is AD
   REGARDLESS of its gnomAD pLI/LOEUF values.

3. The "GNOMAD GENE CONSTRAINT" block (pLI/LOEUF) in the evidence is
   GENE-level LoF-tolerance data, not an inheritance-mode statement by
   itself. Use it only as follows:
   - A LoF-intolerant gene (pLI≥0.9 or LOEUF<0.35) is CONSISTENT with a
     haploinsufficiency-based AD mechanism, but does not by itself prove
     dominance — you still need an affirmative inheritance statement (rule 1).
   - A LoF-tolerant gene (low pLI, high LOEUF) ARGUES AGAINST a
     haploinsufficiency-based AD mechanism specifically — but does NOT rule
     out AD if the evidence separately describes a dominant-negative or
     gain-of-function mechanism (rule 2), and does NOT by itself justify
     reclassifying a gene the evidence otherwise affirmatively describes as
     dominant. Never let pLI/LOEUF alone override an explicit, affirmative
     inheritance-mode statement from the evidence.
   - Do NOT use pLI/LOEUF as a substitute for an inheritance statement in
     either direction (i.e. do not infer AD purely from a low LOEUF, or AR
     purely from a high LOEUF) — constraint metrics inform mechanism
     plausibility, they are not themselves a mode-of-inheritance citation.

4. If the evidence reports the gene as having BOTH dominant and recessive

5. If the evidence is silent, ambiguous, or exclusively about a different
