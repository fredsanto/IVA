If that block also reports no match/no coverage in gnomAD, that IS a legitimate absence-of-data signal — apply PM2 cautiously in that case, noting explicitly that it rests on absence from gnomAD rather than a directly observed low frequency.
- PM5 [Moderate, +2]: Novel missense change at an amino acid residue where a DIFFERENT missense pathogenic variant has been documented at the SAME residue. MISSENSE ONLY, ON BOTH SIDES: (1) THIS variant must be a missense substitution — never apply PM5 to a nonsense/stop-gained, frameshift, in-frame indel, splice or synonymous variant; (2) the cited hit must itself be a missense change — a nonsense/stop-gained (…Ter / *) or frameshift hit at the same residue is NOT a "different missense change" and never supports PM5, even when tagged DIFFERENT amino acid change and pathogenic. CITATION REQUIRED: cite the qualifying hit's own protein change, c. notation or ClinVar Variation ID from the residue-level block; an uncited PM5 line is invalid and will be removed. Definition: same residue number, but a DIFFERENT destination amino acid than this variant's own, regardless of nucleotide change. PRIMARY SOURCE: the "CLINVAR RESIDUE-LEVEL SEARCH" evidence block above already tags each hit at this residue — apply PM5 when a hit is tagged "DIFFERENT amino acid change than this variant" AND flagged [PATHOGENIC/LIKELY PATHOGENIC]; cite that hit's own change (e.g. established p.Xaa120Leu vs. this variant's own p.Xaa120Ser). A hit tagged "SAME amino acid change" is PS1's territory (above), never PM5's. Worked example: candidate p.Xaa120Ser vs. an established Pathogenic p.Xaa120Leu — same residue 120, Ser vs. Leu are different amino acids → PM5, not PS1 (residue number matching is not amino-acid matching).
  PM5 and PS1 may both apply to the same variant if separate hits support each.
- PP3 [Supporting, +1]: Multiple lines of computational evidence support a deleterious effect. Only apply this criterion if the evidence above contains actual named in-silico tool scores (e.g. REVEL, CADD, SIFT, PolyPhen-2, AlphaMissense, SpliceAI). If no in-silico score is present in the evidence, do NOT apply PP3 — a vague "computational evidence supports pathogenicity" without a named tool and score is not sufficient grounding for this criterion.
  When scores ARE present, evaluate each one against its standard pathogenicity threshold below (do not just report the number — judge whether it crosses the deleterious threshold):
    - REVEL: ≥0.7 supports deleterious; ≤0.3 supports benign; in between is inconclusive.
    - CADD (Phred-scaled): ≥20 supports deleterious (top ~1% most damaging); below is weaker/inconclusive.
    - SIFT: <0.05 supports deleterious ("damaging"); ≥0.05 supports tolerated/benign.
    - PolyPhen-2: >0.85 supports deleterious ("probably damaging"); 0.15–0.85 possibly damaging/inconclusive; <0.15 supports benign.
    - AlphaMissense: >0.564 supports deleterious ("likely pathogenic"); <0.34 supports benign ("likely benign"); in between is ambiguous.
    - SpliceAI (delta score): ≥0.5 supports a splice-altering effect; <0.2 does not.
  Apply PP3 only when the reported scores actually cross their deleterious threshold — cite each tool's name and score value (e.g. "REVEL=0.82, CADD=28.3") alongside the judgment. If a score falls in the inconclusive/benign range, say so explicitly and do not count it toward PP3.
- PP4 [Supporting, +1]: Patient's phenotype or family history is highly specific for a disease with a single known genetic etiology. Three conditions must ALL hold — do not apply PP4 on any one or two alone:
  (1) Single-gene etiology: the evidence indicates this disease/phenotype is caused by variants in ONE gene (not genetically heterogeneous — i.e. not a condition where multiple different genes are each independently known to cause the same phenotype). If the evidence shows or does not rule out genetic heterogeneity, do NOT apply PP4 — state the heterogeneity explicitly instead. A real past failure to not repeat: PP4 was applied for a GENE_X variant in a patient with "condition Y and an affected mother" reasoning "specific for condition Y caused by GENE_X variants" — but condition Y is a genetically HETEROGENEOUS disease (GENE_X causes most cases, but two other genes independently cause the identical phenotype too) — condition (1) fails here regardless of how classic the phenotype looks, and PP4 should not have been applied. Before applying PP4, actively check whether the evidence names other genes for the same disease (as this pipeline's own gene evidence table often does, by listing multiple candidate genes for the same phenotype in one run) — if it does, that alone rules out single-gene etiology.
  (2) Discriminating specificity: the patient's phenotype must include at least one feature that is itself a recognized hallmark of this specific disease, not merely a generic symptom this disease happens to share with many other conditions. A broad, non-discriminating overlap (e.g. "intellectual disability", "developmental delay", "seizures", "short stature" in isolation) is NOT sufficient — those are common to hundreds of syndromes and do not distinguish this disease from its differentials. Example of a genuinely specific match: micropenis in a patient being evaluated for Kallmann syndrome — micropenis reflects the congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism that defines Kallmann and is not an incidental or generic finding. Name the exact feature(s) driving the match and state explicitly why they are distinctive for this disease rather than merely present in it. If the only overlap is nonspecific/generic features, do NOT apply PP4 — say so explicitly instead of applying it on general plausibility.
  (3) Full coverage: read the "PHENOTYPE CLUSTER MATCH (backend-extracted from Stage 1 reasoning — authoritative, do not re-derive)" block above. PP4 may only be applied when that verdict is YES. A verdict of PARTIAL means the gene's own literature explains only one cluster of the patient's phenotype and is silent on the rest — that silence is fine for keeping the variant in the report and for PVS1/inclusion purposes (PARTIAL is treated as a sufficient positive link there), but it means the phenotype match is, by definition, not "highly specific" for what this particular patient actually has; a partial match cannot carry PP4 no matter how hallmark-like the matched feature is on its own. Do NOT apply PP4 when the verdict is PARTIAL, NO, or UNKNOWN — only YES satisfies this condition. (If an older-format input has no such backend block at all and phenotype fit must be judged unconstrained from the narrative, apply this same logic to that judgment: only an essentially-complete match satisfies condition (3), not a match to one cluster of a multi-cluster phenotype.)

     If the CLINICAL REASONING above includes a "CLUSTER MATCH:" line, start
     from the exact feature it named rather than re-deriving phenotype fit
     from scratch for condition (2) — but do not let a CLUSTER MATCH: YES by
     itself satisfy condition (2). CLUSTER MATCH only asks whether the
     evidence links this gene to some feature of the phenotype at all (which
     is enough to keep the variant at the triage stage, where a multi-system
     phenotype's unrelated clusters must not count against it); PP4's
     condition (2) asks the stricter question of whether that SAME named
     feature is itself a hallmark distinguishing this disease from its
     differentials, or just a generic finding the CLUSTER MATCH happened to
     key on (e.g. a named feature of "developmental delay" or "intellectual
     disability" passes CLUSTER MATCH but still fails condition (2) here, per
     the disqualifying examples above). Condition (3) above is a separate,
     independent gate on top of this — a feature can pass condition (2)'s
     specificity test and still fail PP4 outright on condition (3) if
     COVERAGE is SUBSET.

  A real past failure to not repeat: the patient's phenotype input was the single generic term "intellectual disability" and PP4 was applied anyway with the justification "a core feature of the [GENE]-associated syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder" — this is circular (every candidate gene in the whole run shares this same generic phenotype; being a "core feature" of a broad syndrome name is not the same as being a discriminating feature) and directly violates this condition's own listed example. A second, near-identical recurrence to also not repeat: the input was "intellectual disability. father and mother unaffected" and PP4 was applied again with the same reasoning — segregation/pedigree facts (parents affected/unaffected, family history of the SAME condition, consanguinity) are NOT clinical/phenotypic features and do NOT count toward condition (2)'s discriminating-feature requirement; they inform inheritance model (de novo vs. inherited) but say nothing about what disease this patient has. Before applying PP4, strip out any segregation/pedigree wording from the phenotype input and check what's left: if what remains is ONLY a generic disease-category term ("intellectual disability", "developmental delay", "seizures", "short stature", etc., alone or in combination with each other) with no actual distinguishing clinical finding, condition (2) fails by construction — do not apply PP4 for ANY gene in that run on phenotype grounds, no matter how strong the gene-disease literature link otherwise looks. PP4 needs a specific clinical finding to point to; a generic diagnosis label plus a pedigree fact is still not one.
  Regardless of how phenotype-specific the match is, the point value used for PP4 must always stay below 5 (Supporting [+1] by default; Moderate [+2] at most if evidence strongly justifies it — never Strong [+4] or higher).
- BA1 [Standalone Benign, -8]: Allele frequency > 5% in population databases.
- BS1 [Strong Benign, -4]: Allele frequency greater than expected for disorder. This requires an explicit comparison between this variant's Frequency and a stated disease prevalence/expected-carrier-frequency figure — the evidence above generally does NOT contain disease-prevalence data (no tool in this pipeline fetches it), so BS1 will usually have no valid grounding and should NOT be applied. Do not apply BS1 merely because the frequency "seems too common" for a rare disease, or from gene/disease familiarity — that is a plausibility judgment, not cited evidence. Only apply if the evidence above explicitly states a disease prevalence or expected-frequency figure to compare against.
- BS2 [Strong Benign, -4]: Observed in a clinically unaffected individual (parent or other relative) at a zygosity/genotype that should cause a fully penetrant disorder. Apply ONLY when BOTH conditions below hold — do not apply on either alone:
  (1) Carrier + unaffected status, DEFAULT-UNAFFECTED POLICY: a parent (or other relative) carries this exact variant — determine zygosity from the Allelic_balance_mother/Allelic_balance_father fields (a non-"NA", non-zero value indicates that parent carries the variant; "0" or "NA" indicates they do not). Unlike most other criteria, this one does NOT require an explicit "unaffected" statement to trigger: treat a carrier parent/relative as UNAFFECTED by default whenever the patient phenotype/family-history text is silent about that parent's own clinical status — most parents presenting a proband for testing are unaffected, and requiring an explicit "healthy"/"unremarkable" statement before ever counting this signal meant BS2 almost never fired in practice, silently discarding a real and common benign signal. Only skip this default — i.e. treat the parent as a poor/non-informative BS2 candidate — when the phenotype/family-history text ITSELF states that parent/relative IS affected, symptomatic, or carries a diagnosis consistent with the condition (e.g. "father with suspected X", "mother also affected"); in that case this parent is not a clean unaffected carrier and BS2 should not be built on them (look for a different carrier relative instead, if any). Silence defaults to unaffected; an explicit statement of the parent being affected is what overrides that default — not the other way around. A HETEROZYGOUS carrier parent is only informative for a gene/mechanism where heterozygosity itself is expected to cause disease (autosomal dominant, or a gene whose literature describes disease typically arising de novo/from a single LoF hit) — for a genuinely autosomal RECESSIVE gene, an unaffected heterozygous carrier parent is the normal, expected, uninformative finding and must NOT trigger BS2 (that is what an unaffected carrier parent of a recessive condition is supposed to look like). A homozygous/compound-heterozygous unaffected relative is the correct signal for a recessive mechanism instead.
  (2) PENETRANCE GATE (mandatory — check before applying, this is the difference between a real BS2 finding and a false one): if the RETRIEVED evidence above explicitly documents, with a citable source (PMID/URL), incomplete/reduced penetrance for THIS gene or condition, an age-dependent onset the unaffected carrier hasn't yet reached, or mosaicism as a known phenomenon for this gene, do NOT apply BS2 at all — an unaffected carrier is the expected, uninformative outcome under any of those, not evidence against pathogenicity. A real worked example (do not repeat the wrong version): literature evidence discussed a related paralog gene as having "incomplete penetrance", and explicitly did NOT make that same claim for the candidate gene itself — that absence of a stated penetrance caveat for the candidate gene (as opposed to it being silent on penetrance generally) is what makes an unaffected carrier parent meaningfully unusual enough to apply BS2, since the gene's own literature otherwise frames its pathogenic variants as typically causing disease.
  CRITICAL — SPECULATION IS NOT A PENETRANCE CITATION: a real past failure to not repeat: the evidence stated the gene's LoF variants typically arise de novo (with a citable PMID), the father was documented as an unaffected carrier of the same variant, and instead of applying BS2 the model wrote "the variant retains its Pathogenic classification... despite the discordant inheritance pattern suggesting potential reduced penetrance or phenocopy in the father" — inventing "reduced penetrance" as an in-the-moment explanation for the discordance, with no citation, then using that self-invented explanation as an excuse to skip BS2 entirely. This is backwards and is a HARD ERROR: the model does not get to hypothesize reduced penetrance or phenocopy on the spot to explain away an unaffected carrier — that hypothesis must already exist as a citable finding in the retrieved evidence (per the worked example above) to exempt BS2. An unaffected-carrier discordance with NO pre-existing citable penetrance/phenocopy explanation is precisely what BS2 exists to capture — apply it. If the evidence is completely silent on penetrance for this specific gene (neither confirms full penetrance nor documents reduced penetrance, and no related-gene contrast is available either), you MUST still apply BS2 (not skip it), and simply flag the penetrance uncertainty in the Comment rather than treating full penetrance as a given.
  HOMOZYGOUS PARENT IS A STRONGER VERSION OF THIS SAME SIGNAL, NEVER "ABSENT": if a parent's Allelic_balance is ~1.0 (roughly ≥0.8), that parent is HOMOZYGOUS for this exact variant — not wild-type, not "absent," not a de novo-supporting negative parent, regardless of how high or low the proband's own AB is. Read the raw number: an AB near 0 means the parent does not carry the variant; an AB near 0.5 means a heterozygous carrier; an AB near 1.0 means a homozygous carrier — these three read in the OPPOSITE direction from "closer to 1.0 = more wild-type," which is a real, documented misreading to never repeat. A homozygous unaffected parent for a variant being evaluated under a dominant/AD-relevant mechanism is BS2 evidence at least as strong as a heterozygous unaffected carrier (arguably stronger — a true dominant/haploinsufficiency mechanism would be expected to manifest at least as severely in a homozygote as in a heterozygote, so an unaffected homozygous parent is a more striking discordance, not a lesser one). Apply BS2 under the same DEFAULT-UNAFFECTED POLICY and PENETRANCE GATE above.
  HARD GATE — PS2 IS BANNED IN THIS SECTION AND A NON-ABSENT PARENT AB CAN NEVER SUPPORT IT ANYWAY: this base-conclusion template already forbids applying PS2 here (see the note near the top of this prompt — de novo status is computed only at the MOI layer stage from this same parental data). A real past failure to not repeat, stacking both errors at once: a variant with Allelic_balance_mother=0 and Allelic_balance_father=1.00 was written up with "PS2 [Strong, +4]: De novo variant confirmed by testing of parents (mother AB=0, father AB=1.00)" — treating the father's AB=1.00 as if it were an absent/negative parent, when AB=1.00 is the single clearest possible signal that the father carries (and is homozygous for) the exact variant in question. This is not a borderline misread; PS2 categorically requires BOTH parents to show absence (AB near 0), and this base-conclusion template is not the place to apply PS2 regardless. The correct write-up recognizes the father as a homozygous carrier and evaluates BS2 (per the paragraph above), not PS2. Before writing any segregation-related criterion here, check both parental AB values against the absent/het/hom bands (near 0 / near 0.5 / near 1.0) and never describe a non-"NA", non-near-zero parental AB as "absent," "negative," "wild-type," or supportive of a de novo claim.
  This is independent of PS2/PM6 (de novo confirmation, computed separately at the MOI layer stage from the same parental data) — BS2 concerns the opposite scenario: a variant INHERITED from a parent who is unaffected, not one confirmed absent in both parents.
- BS4 [Strong Benign, -4]: Lack of segregation with disease in affected family members. Do not apply this to outweigh a strong LoF/PVS1 finding without explicitly reconciling the conflict in the Comment.
- BP6 [Supporting, -1]: A reputable source (ClinVar) reports THIS variant's own classification as Likely benign or Benign. Apply from the **ClinVar:** field above (i.e. this variant's own ClinVar_class, not a different variant/residue) reading Likely benign or Benign. Unlike BS3/PS3, BP6 does NOT require functional-study data — the classification label itself is sufficient grounding; do not withhold BP6 merely because no functional evidence is cited, that is the entire point of this criterion (a reputable source's call, taken on trust, precisely when independent functional evaluation is unavailable). Do not apply if ClinVar_class is Uncertain significance, Pathogenic, Likely pathogenic, or "Conflicting interpretations" (including any Conflicting classification that contains a P/LP submission) without explicitly addressing the conflict in the Comment — a genuinely conflicted record is not a reputable unambiguous benign call.
- PP2 / BP1 — CHECK THIS VARIANT'S OWN Type FIELD FIRST, before reading the verdict: PP2 and BP1 both require this variant's own Type to be missense. The "CLINVAR GENE-LEVEL P/LP VARIANT COUNTS" block's verdict ("missense-predominant" / "nonsense/frameshift-predominant") is a GENE-level fact — true for every variant in that gene regardless of what Type this specific variant is — it is NOT itself a signal that PP2 or BP1 applies to this variant. Worked negative example (a real past failure — do not repeat it): a homozygous NONSENSE variant in a gene with verdict "nonsense/frameshift-predominant" is NOT "a nonsense variant in a truncating-predominant gene, supporting BP1" — that is backwards. A nonsense variant in a truncating-predominant gene is a mechanism MATCH (PVS1 territory, since LoF is confirmed to be how this gene causes disease), never BP1. BP1 fires only when the CANDIDATE ITSELF is missense despite the gene being truncating-predominant (a mechanism MISMATCH, arguing against pathogenicity) — if Type is anything other than missense (nonsense, frameshift, splice, etc.), BP1 cannot apply no matter what the verdict says, full stop.
  - PP2 [Supporting, +1]: Missense variant in a gene with a low rate of benign missense variation and where missense variants are a common mechanism of disease. Only apply if this variant's Type is missense AND verdict is "missense-predominant" — do not apply from gene name, general plausibility, or the verdict text alone without first confirming Type is missense.
  - BP1 [Supporting, -1]: Missense variant in a gene for which primarily truncating variants are known to cause disease. Only apply if this variant's Type is missense AND verdict is "nonsense/frameshift-predominant" — do not apply from gene name, general plausibility, or the verdict text alone without first confirming Type is missense. PP2 and BP1 are mutually exclusive for the same variant.
- BP7 [Supporting, -1]: A synonymous variant for which no splicing disruption is predicted. Only apply if this variant's own Type is synonymous — never apply to missense, nonsense, frameshift, or splice-region variants regardless of their SpliceAI scores. When Type is synonymous, check the "SpliceAI" evidence block for this variant: cite its actual delta scores (DS_AG/AL/DG/DL) as the basis for "no splicing disruption predicted" (all deltas <0.2, per the PP3 SpliceAI threshold above) — do not assert "no disruption predicted" without citing that block. If no SpliceAI evidence is present for this variant, do NOT apply BP7.

Evaluate each criterion based on the evidence in the context. Only list criteria that are supported by evidence or that can be ruled out with evidence.

- Likely Pathogenic: 6 to 9
- Uncertain Significance (VUS): 0 to 5
- Likely Benign: -1 to -6
