EXCLUDED FINDINGS:
The patient phenotype may also state findings the patient does NOT have — worded with "not", "no", "nor", "without", "absence of", "excluding", or any equivalent negation. Those are EXCLUDED FINDINGS. An excluded finding is never one of the patient's findings: it can never make a condition MATCH. Judge it semantically — a synonym or a more specific form of an excluded finding counts as that excluded finding; a broader category that merely contains it does not.

TASK:
For each condition, decide first whether it is EXCLUDED, then whether it covers any part of the patient's present phenotype.

EXCLUDED — the condition's name, or one of its core defining features, is an excluded finding. A core defining feature is one the condition is named or diagnosed for, or one its listed features present as typical — not an occasional or rare one. EXCLUDED takes precedence over MATCH: a condition carrying an excluded finding is EXCLUDED even if it also lists a feature the patient has.
MATCH — not EXCLUDED, and the condition covers a present patient finding: that finding, or a more specific form of it, is the condition's name or one of its listed clinical features — even if it is not the condition's main feature.
NO MATCH — neither of the above.

When a condition has no listed features, judge it by what its name states. The MedGen entry was found by name search: if its title is clearly a different disease from the condition (a different mechanism, inheritance or clotting factor, not merely a synonym or numbered subtype), ignore its features and judge that condition by its name.

Write one line per condition, in this exact form:
- <condition>: MATCH | NO MATCH | EXCLUDED — <at most 15 words: the matching feature, the excluded finding it carries, or why none>

Then ONE verdict line, in exactly this form:
CLUSTER_PHENOTYPE: YES | PARTIAL | INCIDENTAL | EXCLUDED

YES        — at least one MATCH condition covers the patient's present phenotype as a whole.
PARTIAL    — MATCH conditions cover at least one distinct part of the patient's present phenotype, but not all of it.
EXCLUDED   — no condition is MATCH, and at least one condition is EXCLUDED.
INCIDENTAL — no condition is MATCH or EXCLUDED.

One MATCH is enough for YES or PARTIAL, however many other conditions do not match or are EXCLUDED. When the patient phenotype states no excluded finding, never write EXCLUDED.
