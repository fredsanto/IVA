TASK — INCLUSION DECISION:

Decide whether this variant should proceed to the final clinical conclusion.
INCLUDE only variants with real clinical significance: a clear gene-phenotype link
AND probable pathogenicity. Both criteria must be supported by evidence.
Variants included here appear in the final clinical report — be selective.

INCLUDE requires evidence for BOTH:

1. GENE-PHENOTYPE LINK — the gene is known or strongly suspected to cause the
   patient's phenotype:
   - Gene reported in literature or OMIM for this phenotype or a phenotypically
     overlapping condition, OR
   - ClinVar entries for this gene list the patient's disease as associated condition.

2. PROBABLE PATHOGENICITY — the variant itself is likely damaging:
   - ClinVar Pathogenic or Likely pathogenic, OR
   - Strong ACMG criterion applies (PVS1, PS1, PS3, or equivalent), OR
   - Variant is rare (AF < 0.1%) with predicted functional impact (LoF, splice
     disruption, or high CADD score ≥ 20) — ZYGOSITY MUST MATCH INHERITANCE MODE:
     for a known AUTOSOMAL RECESSIVE gene, a single heterozygous LoF variant with
     no second hit (no compound het, not homozygous) is INSUFFICIENT on its own —
     treat as weak evidence, not strong, OR
   - Variant is homozygous, or compound heterozygous with a second variant in the
     same gene, in a known recessive gene with low population frequency, OR
   - Confirmed or assumed DE NOVO (PS2/PM6 — AB data shows absent in both parents,
     or one parent untested) in a gene relevant to the phenotype is a STRONG
     pathogenicity signal, regardless of variant type (missense, LoF, etc.) — rank
     this above an unresolved single het LoF in a recessive gene — BUT only if the
     variant is ALSO rare (AF < 0.1%). De novo status does not override frequency:
     a de novo call on a common variant is not a valid de novo pathogenic signal
     (likely artifact or AB misread) and must not be INCLUDEd on that basis alone, OR
   - Variant or position reported as disease-causing in literature.

   Note: "no functional study" does NOT equal no pathogenicity support. Rarity,
   high CADD, homozygosity, confirmed de novo status, and strong gene-disease link
   are all positive signals even when no wet-lab data exists for this specific variant.

EXCEPTION — Compound heterozygous candidates (autosomal recessive / AD-AR genes only):
   If two or more variants are present in the same gene in this patient, INCLUDE
   if criterion 1 is met, even when individual variant pathogenicity evidence is limited.
   If ALLELIC BALANCE data confirms the variants are in trans (inherited from different
   parents), this strengthens the compound heterozygous case significantly.

   If a "SIBLING VARIANT" block is present below, this gene has an inheritance mode
   that is autosomal recessive (or AD/AR) AND another kept variant exists in the same
   gene — the pipeline has already restricted this block to that situation, so treat
   its presence as confirmation the compound-het scenario applies here.
   - Do NOT EXCLUDE this variant solely because its own individual evidence looks
     weak, IF the sibling variant's evidence (per the sibling's reasoning below) is
     NOT clearly benign — i.e. the sibling is ClinVar Pathogenic/Likely pathogenic/VUS,
     or has no ClinVar entry but is rare with plausible functional impact. Evaluate the
     pair together: two variants in trans in a recessive gene, each individually modest,
     can jointly explain a recessive phenotype.
   - This safeguard does NOT apply, and normal EXCLUDE criteria still govern, if THIS
     variant itself is clearly benign (ClinVar Benign/Likely benign, common AF > 1%,
     no functional impact) — a sibling's status never rescues a variant that is
     independently benign.
   - If phase (cis vs trans) is not confirmed by ALLELIC BALANCE data, do not assume
     cis (same-parent, non-compound) — treat phase as unknown/possibly-trans and still
     apply this safeguard, but note the phase uncertainty in the Include-case reason.
   - HARD RULE — if ALLELIC BALANCE data CONFIRMS both variants were inherited from
     the SAME parent (cis, same allele), this is NOT compound heterozygous — only one
     parental allele carries a variant, the other parental allele is wild-type at both
     positions, so a biallelic/recessive model is NOT satisfied. This safeguard does
     NOT apply in that case; fall back to normal single-heterozygous-in-AR-gene
     criteria for THIS variant (EXCLUDE unless a true second trans hit, homozygosity,
     or independent dominant-mechanism evidence exists).

EXCLUDE if:
   - Gene-phenotype link is absent or only speculative (no literature, no OMIM
     association, no ClinVar disease entries for this phenotype), OR
   - Pathogenicity evidence is truly absent: variant is common (AF > 1%), CADD low,
     no LoF mechanism, ClinVar Benign or Likely benign with no conflicting evidence, OR
   - Single heterozygous variant (any consequence) in a known AUTOSOMAL RECESSIVE
     gene with no second hit and no compound-het evidence — insufficient for
     disease causation under a recessive model, OR
   - Proband allelic balance ≤ 0.2 with no other strong independent evidence
     (ClinVar Pathogenic/Likely pathogenic, confirmed second hit) — treat as an
     unconfirmed/low-confidence call, not sufficient grounds for INCLUDE.

Important: missing tool results (failed retrieval, no rsID, no LitVar hits) do NOT
by themselves justify EXCLUDE. Evaluate the variant-level signals that ARE present
(frequency, CADD, zygosity, gene-disease association) before deciding.

Decision: INCLUDE or EXCLUDE
